    Crowd-safety digital twin for mass gatherings

    Bheed Drishti

    A clay model of a temple town on a river: ghats, narrow lanes, a footbridge, barricades and gates, a railway station. Every figure is one pilgrim walking under a social-force crowd model.

    Two copies of the same town start from the same crowd and receive exactly the same arrivals. On the left, police follow the printed barricade plan. On the right, an AI controller reads the cameras every two seconds, forecasts density three minutes ahead and acts: gates, one-way lanes, a holding ground and PA announcements.

    Above about 4 people per m², a crowd crush becomes possible. We measure the peak density and the minutes any zone spends above that line.

    How it works

    What is under the hood

    Crowd model

    Each pilgrim is a disc (radius 0.20–0.25 m) driven by a social-force model (Helbing & Molnár): a pull toward the next destination along a shortest-path field, exponential repulsion from neighbours and walls, body contact when people touch, and a keep-left bias that lets two-way lanes form. People stuck in a jam grow impatient and push harder, which is how crush densities above 5 p/m² build up.

    Measurements

    Local density is a smoothed head count per square metre. A zone's density is the density its people actually experience (person-weighted). Counter-flow is the share of walkers moving against the zone's main direction. Crowd pressure is density × velocity variance, the turbulence signal found in the 2006 Hajj analysis.

    The two plans

    Fixed plan: the printed barricade plan. Gates never move and nobody is told about a jammed gate or a cordon until they reach it.

    AI-guided: reads zone head counts with ±4 % camera error, forecasts three minutes ahead and acts: one-way lanes (in by Gates F and D, out by B and J), reroutes pilgrims to emptier ghats by PA and signage, holds new arrivals at the Holding Ground and releases them in batches, broadcasts jams and cordons, calms a rumour run, and opens covered shelters in rain.

    The real version

    Live head counts would come from density-map computer vision on CCTV and drone video, with gate, PA and signage control wired to the same loop. This demo replaces the cameras with the simulation's own counts.

    Simulated precinct: 280 × 210 m, 1 m grid, 0.1 s time step, up to ~8,000 pilgrims per plan. Each figure is one person.
